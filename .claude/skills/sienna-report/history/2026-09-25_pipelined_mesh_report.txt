========================================================
All numbers are Verilator RTL simulation on the farm. Latency in time assumes a 950 MHz clock (no synthesis yet).

The one-set-at-a-time design is at tag serial_mesh_v1 in both repos.

What changed
------------
1. Mesh pipelined: sets flow through the arrays back to back. Two operand banks per array, a gap-free skewed feed, PEs keep
   ACC_BANKS=4 banks of 6 partial sums, the reducers combine them in their adder tree, pipelined across sets and restarted
   with no gap (next_final look-ahead). RESULT_BANKS=4. The last host row may come with the start.
2. Credits: SETS_IN_FLIGHT parameter, default 8 (was 3), ids $clog2(SETS_IN_FLIGHT+1) bits.
3. ReLU and linear sets bypass the GPNAE lanes (exact); 1x1 pooling with no padding bypasses FIFO2 and Maxpool.
4. Per-set bias added by the mesh as one more reducer-tree input (no latency); the compiler attaches it to the first pass.
5. TB_sienna_model: streaming host by default (+host_gaps for the old handshake), +trace stage-event log.

Costs to weigh at synthesis
---------------------------
Per PE: 4 banks x 6 partial sums = 24 registers (was 6), plus two operand banks per array (was one). Result memory 4 N^2
words (was 2). One extra adder per output tile for the bias. The per-PE final flags are ANDed across the array each cycle.

Where the remaining time goes
-----------------------------
N=32: none at the mesh; it is at its arithmetic limit (N cycles per set). N=16: about 8% in the activation stage's per-set
overhead and result-bank turnaround. Beyond that the host feed (N words per operand per cycle) and the mesh need the same N
cycles per set, so the next step up needs operands kept on chip (weight cache), and only then does partitioning a large
mesh into smaller ones pay off. Model efficiency is also bounded by the lowering: depthwise layers (KWS, VWW) use ~31-36%
of each set's MAC slots, and batch-1 fully connected layers ~6%.
