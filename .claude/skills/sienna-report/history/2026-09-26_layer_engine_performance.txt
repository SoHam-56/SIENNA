6. Reading it
- Dense layers run close to the mesh's limit: ResNet-8 at N=16 uses 86% of all MAC slots end to end; large GEMMs reach
  93% (N=16) and 99.9% (N=32).
- The biggest loss is padding, a property of how layers map onto N x N sets, not of the pipeline:
  depthwise layers fill 2-6% of a set (one weight per column per tap), batch-1 fully connected layers 1/N of the rows,
  and layers with fewer channels than N (ResNet-8's first stage at N=32) half the columns. That is why N=32 is only
  2.6x faster than N=16 on ResNet-8 despite 4x the PEs, and why KWS, VWW and ad01 sit at 3-30%.
- Per-set overhead is 9-11% at N=16 and 2-3% at N=32. Measured on 2026-09-26 with TB_sienna_layer's stall counter:
  at N=16 it is all credits (934 of 10336 cycles on a ResNet-8-like layer), because SETS_IN_FLIGHT=8 sets of 16
  cycles do not cover a set's start-to-completion time under load; at N=32 there are no credit stalls.
- Per-layer fill and drain is 100-160 cycles, 2-14% of a model; it grows with layer count and matters most for small
  layers (FC, pooling). Running consecutive layers back to back would hide it.
- Host traffic is 15-17 words per cycle at N=16 and 33-54 at N=32 per bus pair, with the weight cache cutting it by
  32-45% on convolutional models.
- What would move the numbers next, largest first: a depthwise mode that packs channels (KWS/VWW 3-10x on those layers),
  partitioning N=32 into smaller meshes for narrow layers, overlapping consecutive layers, and batching fully connected
  layers.
